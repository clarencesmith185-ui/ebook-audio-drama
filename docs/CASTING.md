# Role-first voice casting

Before designing a new voice, record:

1. Canonical identity and scene-local aliases; whether the person actually speaks.
2. Scene age / life stage, only as specifically as source evidence permits.
3. Narrative role and any explicitly stated vocal traits.
4. Source anchors supporting those facts.
5. Artistic choices: pitch, texture, cadence, clarity and contrast with existing voices.

A design description is not a biography. Do not infer guilt, innocence, nationality, disability, accent or personality from a name or occupation. Separate explicit source traits from invented casting choices. Contrast two similar characters by timbre and rhythm, not exaggerated stereotypes.

Example:

> Adult café attendant, medium register, warm slightly textured voice, clear conversational delivery and a brisk but relaxed rhythm. Distinct from the lower-register narrator.

Role/evidence: the source explicitly identifies an adult café attendant greeting a customer. Warmth, register and texture are artistic choices, not source facts.

Use a neutral original reference transcript, not a scene-specific cry or whisper, as the stable identity anchor. Reuse the generated WAV and exact transcript across chapters. Seeds and descriptions alone do not recreate an identical voice. Record sample quality separately; a generated sample is not an approved one.

For unnamed characters, use scene-grounded IDs until there is evidence that two appearances are the same person. Do not merge every police officer, waiter or customer into one voice automatically.
